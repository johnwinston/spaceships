Conway's
Game of Life

John Horton Conway · 1937–2020

A "game" with no players. Set up some live cells on a grid, then watch: every generation, each cell lives or dies by counting its neighbours. Martin Gardner published it in Scientific American in October 1970, and people have been finding new things in it ever since.

Photo: Thane Plambeck, via Wikimedia Commons

The rules

Count the
neighbours

3 neighbours: born. 2 or 3: stays. Otherwise: gone.

Every cell updates at the same moment. That's the whole game, written B3/S23: born with 3, survives with 2 or 3.

Try it

Draw. Play.

Tap cells to draw, then press Play. Or load the R-pentomino and watch it run.

R-pentomino

The first
natural glider

Five cells. At generation 69, something walks away.

The R-pentomino churns for 1,103 generations before it settles. In 1970 Richard Guy spotted a piece breaking off and walking across the board: the first glider anyone had seen. Nobody designed it.

Spaceships

Patterns
that fly

A spaceship returns to its own shape, shifted over, and keeps going forever. Conway found the lightweight, middleweight and heavyweight ships in 1970. They move at c/2, half the "speed of light" of one cell per generation. The glider moves at c/4.

Different rules

Same cells,
new universe

Conway's rule · ship p4-01's rule · ship p4-24's rule

Change which neighbour counts cause a birth or a survival and you get a different universe. The ships in this collection use rules that also look at where the neighbours are, not just how many.

No formula

Every ship is
searched for

There is no formula that produces spaceships. The only way to know what a pattern does is to run it, and there are far too many patterns to try them all. So searches build ships one row at a time, and throw away rows that can't work.

Partials

Half a ship flies, then falls apart. ikpx2 grows partials until one closes.

Sir Robin, the first ship that moves like a chess knight, was found in 2018 by Adam P. Goucher by extending a partial. Here it is cut after 20, 38 and 56 rows, next to the whole ship. You can hand ikpx2 the head of a partial and it will keep searching from there.
